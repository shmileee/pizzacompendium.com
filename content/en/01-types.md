## Pizza types to start with {#pizza-types}

### Napoletana {#napoletana}

**Napoletana** – also known as Neapolitan pizza – has risen but fairly low edges with characteristic leopard spotting (dark flecks) and a thin, crêpe-like base.

- Dough [hydration](#terms) is usually 55–60% (some bakers go to 70% or more – the “Canotto” style).
- It is baked in ovens hotter than 400°C, and the bake takes 60–90 seconds.
- [Maturation and rising](#rising-vs-maturation) at room temperature usually take about 8–12 h (depending on the flour strength), while the current rules of the AVPN association (Associazione Verace Pizza Napoletana) call for 12–24 h in total.

### Classica {#classica}

**Classica** – the name this guide uses for the classic pizza with a crunchier crust and a stiffer base than Napoletana. This comes from a longer bake at a lower temperature and from the fat (olive oil or another oil) added to the dough: it makes the dough more tender and more extensible, and adds a little flavour.

- For Classica, flours of [strength](#flour-strength) from about W220 (with a shorter maturation) to W310 are suitable; the basic recipe is written for a flour of medium strength (W250–310).
- Dough [hydration](#terms) is 55–58% (use a higher one once you have some experience).
- The bake takes 2–3 min when the stone or oven is at 300–350°C. In a home oven, bake on a [stone](#faq-stone-setup) right under the top heating element (about 7–12 cm from it) in grill mode: the thermostat usually reaches only 250–270°C, but the glowing element heats the stone further, and the bake then takes about 2–5 min (up to about 7 min in a weaker oven).
- Choose this one for your first pizza: the [basic recipe](#recipe) is written for it.

### Teglia alla Romana {#teglia}

**Teglia alla Romana** – Roman pizza baked in a pan. It has a rectangular shape, a very crunchy and light crust and a thicker profile. It is definitely worth trying.

- Flours of [strength](#flour-strength) W320 and higher are suitable for this pizza.
- Dough hydration is 75–80%.
- The recommended time for the dough to mature and rise is 24–48 h, using the fridge.
- The bake is often in two stages: first you par-bake the base on its own, then you bake it again with the remaining ingredients.
- The baking temperature is about 300°C in a pizza oven; in a home oven set the maximum (usually 250–270°C) and bake a little longer, about 12–15 min.

It is best to bake it in a blue steel pan, which heats up quickly, so the base browns nicely and turns crunchy; to start with, an ordinary metal tray without a non-stick coating will also do. Many people find that this pizza tastes even better reheated, because the base and edges become crunchier. You can reheat slices in an ordinary oven (about 200°C, a few minutes) or in a frying pan.

There are many other types of Italian pizza, including Tonda alla Romana, Pala alla Romana (al Metro), [Pinsa Romana](#guide-pinsa) (an oval flatbread made from a flour blend), Sicilian pizza and Fritta (deep-fried). For a side-by-side comparison, see [Pizza types compared](#pizza-guides).

## Glossary {#terms}

**Bulk dough** – kneaded dough in the form of one large ball.

**Dough ball** – a portion of dough for one pizza. A dough ball for a Napoletana pizza usually weighs about 200–290 g (pizza diameter 22–35 cm), about 270–300 g in the Canotto style. A dough ball for a Classica pizza usually weighs about 220–250 g (pizza diameter 28–33 cm), because this pizza has a smaller rim.

**Balling** – dividing the bulk dough into dough balls, one for each pizza.

**Hydration** – how much water the dough contains, i.e. the ratio of water to flour (for example, 60% means 60 g of water per 100 g of flour).

**Flour strength (W)** – a measure of the “strength” of the gluten in a flour (the result of a laboratory test with an alveograph), given mainly on Italian flours. The higher the W, the more water the flour usually absorbs and the longer the dough holds up while rising. [More about flour strength](#flour-strength).

**Room temperature** – in this guide about 20–23°C. A lower temperature lengthens the rising time and a higher one shortens it.

**The fridge** – about 3–5°C. Do not set it above 5°C just to speed the dough up. The lower the temperature, the more slowly the yeast works and the slower the processes in the dough; in the fridge the dough rises only a little, while maturation carries on, though slowly.

**Rising** – the increase in the volume of the dough, caused by the yeast. [Rising and maturation – the difference](#rising-vs-maturation).

**Maturation** – slower changes in the dough that give it flavour and aroma and make it stretch better.

**Cornicione** – the rim (edge) of the pizza.

**Leopard spotting** – dark spots on the rim of a Neapolitan pizza.

**Biga and poolish** – preferments, i.e. “starters” added to the dough. [Biga and poolish explained](#faq-biga-poolish).

**Autolyse** – a short rest of the flour with water before the salt and yeast are added. [What autolyse is](#faq-autolyse).

**AVPN** – Associazione Verace Pizza Napoletana, an association founded in Naples in 1984 that certifies pizzerias baking Neapolitan pizza to its own written rules (the “disciplinare”).

**Fior di latte** – mozzarella made from cow’s milk. **Mozzarella di bufala** – made from buffalo milk; this cheese usually releases more water on a pizza. [More about cheeses](#mozzarella).

**Pelati and polpa** – tinned tomatoes: pelati are peeled (whole or in pieces), polpa is finely chopped tomato. [How to prepare the sauce](#faq-tomato-sauce).

**Grill mode** (some ovens call it “broil”) – an oven setting in which the top heating element works at full power; in this guide it is the recommended setting for baking pizza on a stone ([how to set up the stone](#faq-stone-setup)). Not every oven allows it with the door closed: check the manual.

**Re-balling** – reshaping a dough ball that has completely lost its structure after sitting too long at room temperature or in the fridge. Use this method only as a last resort, not as a regular part of the rising process.

**Dusting flour** – used when shaping the pizza so that the dough does not stick to the work surface and the peel. It can be flour, semolina (semola) or a special dusting mix. [Which dusting flour works best](#faq-dusting).

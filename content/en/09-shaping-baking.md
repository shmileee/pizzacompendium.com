## Shaping and baking pizza {#shaping-baking}

### Dividing the dough and shaping the dough balls {#balling}

1. Take the bulk dough out of the fridge and wait 1–2 h (2 h in the schedule) until it warms up a little.
2. Lift the dough out of the container carefully with a spatula (do not tear it out) and place it on the work surface.
3. Divide the dough into portions with the spatula and weigh them (240 g each in the example).
4. Shape each portion into a ball: pull the edges of the dough towards the centre and press, turning the portion. Then place it seam side down and make a few circular movements with your hand over the work surface until the ball is smooth and taut on top. A well-closed ball has a smooth top, with the seam sealed underneath ([photo of a well-closed ball](#faq-closed-ball)).
5. Place the balls in an airtight container, leaving gaps between them because they will grow. Do not dust them with flour or oil them. Close the container and leave it at room temperature for 4–5 h (about 20–23°C; less on hot days, and up to about 5–6 h if the room is cooler than about 20°C).
6. About an hour before baking, switch on the oven with the stone ([how to set it up](#faq-stone-setup)): stone on the highest shelf, grill mode at the maximum temperature, everything else taken out of the oven. If you are switching on grill mode at the maximum for the first time, first read the “Hot oven” warning below. Prepare the toppings in the meantime.

The ball is ready for baking when it is clearly bigger, soft and airy, but has not spread out. If it starts to deflate and spread, do not wait any longer. More: [how to tell when a dough ball is ready](#faq-ball-ready).

### Shaping the dough disc {#shaping}

1. Check that the stone is already hot (see [heating the stone](#faq-stone-setup)) and prepare the toppings before you start shaping the pizza.
2. Only now roll the ball in dusting flour (flour, semolina or a special dusting mix) – just enough so that it does not stick to the work surface.
3. Place the ball on the work surface and press it with your fingertips from the centre towards the edges, turning the dough. **Leave an untouched rim of about 1–2 cm.** This is the cornicione, where the air is meant to stay.
4. Stretch the dough disc to a diameter of 30–33 cm by passing the dough between your hands, or by stretching it over the backs of your hands and turning it.
5. **Do not use a rolling pin** – it pushes out the air the dough gathered while rising.
6. If the dough keeps springing back, cover it and let it rest for 15 minutes, then carry on. If a small hole appears, pinch it shut and leave that spot alone.

Experienced bakers also shape Napoletana with the “slap” method: they toss the dough between their hands, slapping it lightly against the palm. This method is optional.

### Toppings and baking {#baking}

::: warning Hot oven
The stone and the heating element are extremely hot, and the element is right above the stone (usually 7–12 cm). Wear oven gloves, do not touch the stone or the element, open the door with your face turned away and leave the hot stone in the oven to cool down. Before you use grill mode at the maximum setting for the first time, check in the oven’s manual whether it may be used with the door closed and for about an hour without a break. If the manual limits continuous grilling (some manufacturers give about 30 minutes), heat the stone first on top-and-bottom heat at the maximum temperature and switch on the grill only for the last 10 minutes or so. Do not put baking paper on the stone: most baking paper is rated for no more than about 220–230°C, so on a stone this hot it chars and can catch fire. Slide the pizza straight from the peel.
:::

1. Place the dough disc on a peel sprinkled with a thin layer of semolina or flour. Too much dusting flour burns on the hot stone and leaves burnt specks on the bottom of the pizza.
2. Rock the peel to check that the dough disc slides freely on it. If it sticks, gently lift it and sprinkle a little semolina or flour underneath.
3. Add the toppings quickly, because the dough gradually sticks to the peel. Spread a thin layer of sauce, then add the mozzarella and the remaining ingredients (about 60–80 g of sauce and 80–100 g of mozzarella for a 30–33 cm pizza). Do not overdo the amount of toppings, and use watery ingredients (e.g. tomatoes) sparingly, because the water comes out during baking.
4. Pop the larger air bubbles on the rim ([about popping bubbles](#faq-bubbles)).
5. Slide the pizza onto the stone with one quick forward movement of the peel, then pull the peel back.
6. Bake according to the [baking times](#faq-bake-times): in a home oven with the stone under the grill usually about 2–5 min (up to about 7 min in a weaker oven). Start checking after 2 min and watch the pizza, not the clock; if the oven heats unevenly, turn the pizza. The pizza is done when the cheese has melted, the rim is browned and, when you lift an edge with the peel, the underside is browned and firm.
7. Remove the pizza with the peel. You can add delicate toppings such as rocket, raw cured ham (for example Parma ham) or fresh basil after baking.
8. Shape and bake further pizzas one at a time, and keep the remaining dough balls closed in the container until you shape them.

## Pizza flours {#popular-flours}

Below is a list of pizza flours. Treat it as a guide, not as a rigid rule: the W and protein values come from the data sheets of manufacturers or sellers and may differ between batches, and the remarks on use are based on the experience of home pizza makers.

::: tip How to choose a flour?
Match the [flour strength (W)](#flour-strength) to the planned maturation time of the dough. To start with, choose a hydration of 55–58% (the lower part of the 55–62% range set for Napoletana in the AVPN rules) and check on the packaging or in the manufacturer’s technical data sheet how much hydration the flour can take. Flours of medium strength (W250–310) work best, for example those marked below as being for beginners.
:::

::: tip
**Storing larger amounts of flour.** Small bags of flour can stay in their original packaging in a cool, dry place, and once opened it is best to pour the flour into an airtight container. A larger 5–20 kg sack is also worth keeping in such a container: put the whole sack in it or pour some of the flour into it. The container should be intended for contact with food (look for that wording or the glass-and-fork symbol). This protects the flour from moths and other insects that like dry food.
:::

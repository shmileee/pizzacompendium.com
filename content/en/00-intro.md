Good pizza takes time – the dough matures for anything from a few hours to two or three days – but it needs little hands-on work. Your first pizza starts the day before: the dough needs about 24 hours, and an ordinary home oven is enough.

## Your first pizza at a glance {#quick-start}

The shortest route to a first pizza in an ordinary home oven. Every point links to the full explanation.

**What you will make.** Four [Classica](#classica) pizzas, 30–33 cm across, from four dough balls of 240 g ([recipe](#recipe)). This pizza works well in an ordinary oven, so start with it; [Napoletana](#napoletana) needs an oven above 400°C, and Teglia alla Romana needs a dough with a very high [hydration](#terms) and a baking pan. You do not need a pizza oven ([which oven is enough](#faq-first-oven)).

**What to have ready:**

- pizza flour of medium strength (about 600 g, plus a little extra flour for dusting), salt, olive oil or another oil, and fresh yeast ([how to choose the flour](#faq-starter-flours); [dry yeast](#faq-yeast-types));
- peeled or chopped tomatoes in a tin, mozzarella and fresh basil ([sauce, cheese and toppings](#toppings));
- a kitchen scale and a jewellery scale accurate to 0.01 g ([or a way to manage without it](#faq-yeast-no-scale)), a bowl, a container with a tight lid, a putty-knife-style spatula and oven gloves ([equipment](#accessories));
- a pizza stone and a peel (a flat paddle for sliding the pizza into the oven), or an upside-down baking tray without a non-stick coating (heated in the oven along with it), or the [frying-pan method](#faq-combo-method).

**The schedule.** The dough needs 24 hours from the end of kneading to the oven, and it spends almost all of that time resting. Count back from the time you want to bake:

| When | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| 24 h before | [Knead the dough](#kneading), shape it into one ball (the bulk dough) and leave it in a closed container at room temperature for 2 h |
| 22 h before | Put the bulk dough in the fridge for 16 h |
| 6 h before | Take the bulk dough out of the fridge and leave it for 2 h |
| 4 h before | [Divide the dough](#balling) into four balls of 240 g and leave them in a closed container at room temperature |
| 1 h before | Put the stone in the oven and switch it on at the maximum temperature: stone on the highest shelf, grill mode, everything else taken out ([how to set it up](#faq-stone-setup); first read the [Hot oven](#baking) warning) |
| Baking | [Stretch the dough](#shaping), add the toppings and [bake](#baking) one pizza at a time, about 2–5 min each |

For example, to start baking on Saturday at 19:00, finish kneading on Friday at 19:00.

**A pizza is done** when the cheese has melted, the rim is browned and the underside, checked by lifting an edge with the peel, is browned and firm ([baking times](#faq-bake-times)).

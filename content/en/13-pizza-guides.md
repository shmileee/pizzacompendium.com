## Pizza types compared {#pizza-guides}

*The recommendations and descriptions below are not rigid rules. They are a loose interpretation drawn from the practice of home pizza makers. Some of these pizza types have their own “disciplinare” (for example AVPN for Napoletana), a document that sets out what a particular pizza should look like.*

| Type | Hydration | Flour (strength W) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Napoletana | 55–60% (with higher hydration up to about 67%; Canotto: 70% and more) | W250–350 and higher, depending on the style |
| Classica | 55–58% | from about W220 (with a shorter maturation); the basic recipe: W250–310 |
| Teglia alla Romana | 75–80% | usually W320 and higher (or protein from about 13.5 g per 100 g) |
| Pinsa Romana | 75–80% | a flour blend or a strong wheat flour |

| Type | Maturation and rising | Baking |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Napoletana | about 8–12 h at room temperature (AVPN: 12–24 h) | above 400°C, 60–90 s |
| Classica | about 24 h (basic recipe) | 2–3 min at 300–350°C; in a home oven about 2–5 min |
| Teglia alla Romana | 24–48 h, mostly in the fridge | about 300°C; in a home oven about 250–270°C and roughly 12–15 min |
| Pinsa Romana | usually at least 24 h, often 48–72 h | about 300°C; in a home oven about 250°C, in two stages |

### Napoletana at a glance {#guide-napoletana}

Key information:

- Classic Napoletana, the “taste of Naples”: hydration of about 55–60%, a dough ball weight of about 250–280 g, and a low, not too puffy cornicione (edge).
- Napoletana with higher hydration: 60–67%, a dough ball weight of 250–290 g, and edges that rise more and are airier.
- Canotto-style Napoletana: hydration of 70% or more, a dough ball weight of 270–300 g, and very high, airy edges. An increased amount of yeast, or even a biga preferment, is often used so that the edges can rise more.
- The dough contains no fat and no sugar.
- Authentic Napoletana is baked at temperatures above 400°C. A pizza baked at lower temperatures (in a home oven) can be called Neapolitan-style.
- Starting proportions: see the [basic recipe](#recipe) (its 55% hydration is within the Napoletana range of 55–60%); leave out the fat.
- You can knead the dough and leave it as a bulk dough, then shape the dough balls a few hours before baking. Alternatively, shape the dough balls right after kneading and give them time to rise.

### Classica at a glance {#guide-classica}

Key information:

- Kneading and shaping are the same as for Napoletana – see [kneading](#kneading) and [shaping](#shaping-baking).
- Add fat (oil or olive oil) to the dough during kneading: in a mixer preferably towards the end, when kneading by hand together with the water or when combining the ingredients ([when to add fat](#faq-fat)).
- A dough ball should weigh about 220–250 g, because the edges rise less.
- The bake is longer and at a lower temperature than for Napoletana – see [baking times](#faq-bake-times).
- The pizza is crunchier than Napoletana and its bottom is firmer.
- You can bake it in a home oven.

### Teglia alla Romana at a glance {#guide-teglia}

The main characteristic is a crunchy, light crust (a pizza baked in a pan).

Key information:

- Best baked in a blue steel pan (prepare a new pan before first use as the maker instructs, and oil it lightly before baking). A 30×40 cm pan takes roughly 600 g of dough.
- Goal: the bulk dough should increase its volume about 3× over the whole maturation, and the dough ball should roughly double its volume before baking.
- Yeast: about 5 g of fresh yeast per 1 kg of flour (usually 2–6 g, depending on temperature and maturation time).
- Approximate times for this kind of dough: about 1–2 h at room temperature (before the fridge), 24–30 h in the fridge, then 3–4 h at room temperature in a tightly closed container (for high-strength flours, W320 and higher).
- The fridge should be at about 4°C. The higher the temperature, the faster the dough gains volume, but do not set the fridge above 5°C just to speed the dough up. Fridge temperature varies, because the fridge warms up every time it is opened.
- You can make the dough with the **no-knead** method (no machine kneading): you only combine the ingredients, after an hour of rest you do several sets of folds about every 15 minutes (in each set you fold the dough in thirds, like a letter, a few times), and the gluten network finishes developing during the long maturation.
- The bake is often in two stages: first the base alone or with sauce only, then the remaining toppings; for the total baking time see [baking times](#faq-bake-times).

### Pinsa Romana at a glance {#guide-pinsa}

A modern Roman variety of pizza with an oval shape, created in 2001 by Corrado Di Marco. The often repeated story of ancient Roman roots is a marketing myth. Pinsa is usually seen as even lighter than Teglia alla Romana, with crunchy edges and a soft, delicate middle.

Key information:

- Dough ball weight: 250 g.
- Oval shape.
- Two-stage bake: in a home oven with a stone heated under the grill, first the base for about 3 min, then the base with toppings for about 2–3 min (without a stone the bake takes longer – judge by looks; in a pizzeria oven the base takes at most about 2 min).
- The dough rises and matures in the fridge for most of the time. Dough balls are usually shaped from cold dough, which sticks less.
- Flour: most often a suitable blend of flours (wheat, rice, soy), but a strong wheat flour can also be used. Producers offer ready-made blends that, besides the flours listed, usually contain dried wheat sourdough. Blends for Pinsa are produced by, among others, the company Di Marco and the mills Polselli (“La Romana”, for Pinsa and pizza in pala), Molino Iaquone (“Per pizza alla pala e pinsa”) and Dallagiovanna (“La Pinsa”).
- An example recipe for a Pinsa blend (approximate values – check the instructions on the pack): 1000 g blend, 800 g very cold water, 20 g salt, 20 g fat, about 7 g fresh yeast (for a 24–48 h maturation). First leave the dough at room temperature until it doubles in volume, then 24–48 h in the fridge as a bulk dough and about 3 h after the fridge as dough balls.
